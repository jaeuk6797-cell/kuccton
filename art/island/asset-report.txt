SUP floating island — opaque holographic opal revision
2026-11-07 (Asia/Seoul)

Scope and baseline

Working branch: codex/island-refinement. Baseline: 32cf717.
The asymmetric outline, thin metal rim, peripheral crystal clusters, ring,
vegetation distribution and open planting zone are preserved. The static asset
contains no flowers, buds, petals or flower placeholders. Flowers in app QA
screenshots are existing runtime content, not part of island.glb.

maximum horizontal diameter to 8 and translates the top to Y=.17. The shared
floating parent moves flowers and island together.

Opaque opal material

Large painted turquoise/lavender/pink ground gradients were removed. A nearly
uniform BDDCD9 milky-aqua base has only minute DFD9E7 undertone variation and
fine grain. Vertex colors are linear, with white exported baseColorFactor;
there is no duplicate sRGB conversion.

Native exported ground/basin material: MeshPhysicalMaterial-compatible PBR,
roughness .18, metalness .02, IOR 1.5, clearcoat .55, coat roughness .10,
iridescence .18 at 410 nm, opacity 1, transmission 0, emission 0. The ground
uses OPAQUE alpha mode and no refraction pass. A single packed 256-square
Non-Color normal map provides subtle mineral platelet orientation at strength
.20. Its clearcoat normal remains smooth. No new geometry supports the effect.

Standard Physical shading was evaluated first. Its highlights moved, but
changing native iridescence .18 to .24 changed the tested center pixel by less
than one RGB byte. A small ground/basin-material onBeforeCompile extension therefore
adds bounded pastel angular scatter to indirect diffuse/specular lighting.
It uses reflected world direction and a Schlick-style grazing term, without
UV/world-position painting, time animation, emission, additional textures,
passes or transparent layers. Direct lighting, shadow response and clearcoat
remain native. A per-material guard prevents duplicate hooks; a stable program
cache key selects the shader program. Native thin-film iridescence is disabled
only on this runtime material because the angular extension supplies the visible
holographic shift. Its exported .18 fallback remains available in other viewers.

This additional angular scatter exists in Island3D.jsx, not in glTF. External
viewers retain the native opaque PBR/clearcoat/iridescence fallback. Blender's
Cycles preview is an offline material preview, not a screenshot of Three.js.

Warm mineral underside

The existing 1,089-triangle faceted underside is unchanged geometrically.
Rose-brown AC8984, warm taupe C1ADA0 and muted mauve A18A9F replace the dominant
cool base. Cyan 63C3C8 and lavender A78AD0 inclusions follow an exposed dipping
bed and oblique fracture; occasional D6A1BB pink joins upper rear facets.
Area-weighted inclusion strength is approximately 17.67%. No separate attached crystal
meshes, extra materials or realistic dirt textures were added.

Lighting and reflections

Neutral Tone Mapping remains at exposure 1. Hemisphere intensity .38, primary
sun 1.5, cyan directional fill .65 and environment intensity .55 are unchanged.
Only the primary sun casts shadows. Existing soft flower/contact shadows and
pastel visible sky remain intact; no Bloom or additional render pass was added.

Four soft elliptical sky cards are captured once into the existing 256 PMREM.
Rear placement covers the flat ground's reflected rays as the camera orbits.
Pink, cyan and lavender cards provide color, with a narrow warm-white key for
polish. Radiance multipliers are 7.875 / 8.75 / 8.75 / 14, and initial angular
blur .03 preserves contrast. The cards and capture resources are disposed
immediately after capture; only the filtered environment remains at runtime.
Brighter card radiance is local reflected light, not higher global exposure.

The final .20 normal strength was selected over .55 because stronger normals
made the reflected highlights look like conspicuous patches. Smooth clearcoat
and restrained base-normal variation produce the quieter polished result.
The current app shows aqua at default, cyan at one orbit angle and lavender/
pink at the opposite/grazing angle. Flowers retain readable color and shape.

Feature cost and angular response

A same-context alternating feature test used 60 frames per block after warmup.
Island-only median frame intervals were 38.6 / 37.8 / 38.5 ms with native
iridescence and 34.7 / 34.8 / 35.1 ms with it disabled. This is approximately
9% less frame time (mean of block medians: 38.30 to 34.87 ms) for the selected runtime material. The custom angular
scatter and clearcoat remain enabled. A Standard-material diagnostic measured
32.5 ms, but was not selected because polished material quality is the goal.
These are Chromium ANGLE SwiftShader software-rendering observations, not
physical mobile GPU benchmarks.

The native-on/off orbit comparison differed by at most 1.9/255 at the fixed
center sample. With the selected native-off shader, that same surface point
changes from aqua [215,237,242] at default to cyan [190,228,241] at -30 degrees
and lavender [199,201,240] at +30 degrees. Alpha is 255 in every sample. This
demonstrates view-dependent coloration with unchanged underlying vertex color.
The tested center highlights remain below clipping.

Matched baseline/final rendering comparison

A separate baseline -> final -> final -> baseline run on the same SwiftShader
context, viewport and scene compared block medians after warmup:

Scene                 Baseline ms       Final ms       Difference
Existing flowers          154.45          157.80            +2.17%
Island only               101.30          105.75            +4.39%

These absolute times belong to this paired window. The software renderer ran
in a different speed regime during the earlier feature test; raw times from
the two windows must not be combined. The matched result shows a modest shader
cost for the redesigned ground, not a claimed universal speedup from fewer
triangles. No physical Android/iOS GPU benchmark was available.

Draw calls remain 405 with runtime flowers and 26 for the island-only app view.
Rendered triangles including existing shadow/output passes fall from 72,116
to 69,944 with flowers, and 26,220 to 24,048 island-only. Static asset geometry
is independently 16,607 triangles. No load or shader/console errors appeared;
the existing THREE.Clock deprecation warning remains.

Remaining limits

The lightweight ground extension approximates pastel mineral scattering; it
is not spectral opal optics. Sky reflections are prefiltered and static in
world space, and do not reflect live flowers. They respond to camera movement
without dynamic cubemap capture. The native GLB fallback and offline Cycles
preview differ from the final app shader. Existing alpha-crystal rendering
retains its normal transparency-ordering limitations. Future Three upgrades
should recheck the shader-chunk integration. Physical mobile GPU performance
and on-device visual fidelity remain unmeasured.

The final asset is intentionally flowerless, with the central region reserved
for existing runtime planting. No application data model, audio, authentication
or interaction code was changed in this revision.
