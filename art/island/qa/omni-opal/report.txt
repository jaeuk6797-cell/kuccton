Diagnosis

The previous four bright reflection cards all occupied the negative-Z sky.
Front views reflected those cards, while side/rear rays sampled the nearly
uniform environment background. The material retained hue but lost the
reflection contrast that makes it read as polished opal. This was not a tone
mapping or color-space bug.

The old tint also collapsed direction to reflection.x + .15*reflection.z.
Its two smoothstep ramps saturated across broad side arcs, reducing dynamic
color separation. The fifth-power grazing term was only about .092 at the
default 22.3-degree elevation. Existing diffuse/specular tint floors were already
.45/.65, so lack of a floor alone did not explain the problem.

Shader refinement

Three continuous circular weights blend aqua, pink and lavender. For a unit
reflected direction each weight is nonnegative, and their sum is one. There
is no angle wrapping, threshold plateau, fixed spatial gradient, screen-space
overlay or time animation. Existing normal-map orientation affects the reflected
direction without modifying normal or texture data.

A quiet milky-aqua pearl multiplier persists in indirect diffuse lighting.
A squared grazing-angle term broadens the additional reflected-color response.
The native PBR Fresnel, direct lighting, contact shadows and clearcoat paths
remain untouched. Alpha remains 1, transmission 0 and ground emission 0. The
existing application choice to disable redundant native ground thin-film
iridescence remains in place; the GLB's native PBR fallback is unchanged.

The reachable high camera angle also needs an upper-view specular response.
Four localized cardinal lobes are reduced algebraically to a single scalar
expression and a twelfth power using four multiplies. The response is gated
above approximately 27 degrees of reflected elevation and fully active near
51 degrees. It multiplies existing indirect specular light, with no emission
or changes to diffuse/direct lighting or clearcoat. The maximum gain is 32;
ordinary front and low viewing directions remain outside its gate. This is a
stylized material response, not a new scene light or realtime reflection.

The scalar form matched the four-vector formula across 10,000 unit directions.
Compared with baseline, the hook uses one fewer smoothstep and two fewer mix
calls, plus bounded scalar polynomial arithmetic for the upper response. It
adds no fragment texture lookup, trigonometric or pow call, loop, texture,
uniform animation, full-screen pass or runtime geometry. The shader cache key
advances to soop-opal-reflection-v3.

A broad upper-sky fill was tested and discarded: it softened flower shadows
without producing enough localized reflection contrast. The final environment
retains the nine accepted horizon cards; the upper response affects only the
shared ground/basin material.

Environment refinement

Nine soft elliptical cards now cover the full horizontal orbit. A narrower
warm-white key keeps a moving highlight; colored side/front/rear bands preserve
pastel reflection contrast. The low pink band covers the lower camera angle.
Four diagonal/side bands use 12 x 3.8 dimensions so their soft falloffs overlap
around the previously weak 60/300-degree directions.

These cards are temporary capture geometry, disposed after the single startup
PMREM capture. The 256 environment resolution, .03 blur and retained texture
allocation remain unchanged. The increase from 4 to 9 cards adds startup capture
work and fill; it does not add draws or geometry to ordinary frames. Existing
resource cleanup disposes their shared geometry and materials once.

Neutral Tone Mapping, exposure 1, environment intensity .55, direct lights,
shadow settings, clearcoat and visible sky are unchanged. There is no Bloom,
refraction pass, per-frame environment capture or camera-following light.

The small mixed difference is consistent with software-renderer variation;
it does not demonstrate a mobile GPU speedup or regression. Draw calls remain
353 with flowers and 26 island-only. The current merged scene contributes 327
dynamic-content calls. Geometry allocations 68, textures 7 and programs 11 are
unchanged. There are no new runtime passes or environment captures.

No shader compilation or asset-loading failures appeared. The existing
THREE.Clock deprecation warning remains. Production build passed, with the
existing large-bundle warning. The larger startup card capture has extra
initialization work; its cost was not separately benchmarked on mobile hardware.

Remaining limitations

Physical Android/iOS GPU performance and display fidelity remain unmeasured.
Upper-angle sheen is deliberately soft; this revision does not promise equally
sharp reflections at every angle. The angular color/scatter and upper specular
response are stylized material approximations, not spectral opal simulation.
The environment is static and does not reflect live flowers. The GLB itself
is unchanged, so external viewers retain its existing native PBR fallback.
Future Three.js upgrades should recheck the shader-chunk integration.
